How it fits

Every sale feeds the network, and every shoot feeds the ads.

Sales bring in the cash and briefs that make the network worth joining. Booked creators produce the proof the ads run on, and the ads bring in the next sales calls.

1Creator Network app: launch at Stage 1

Right now the network has no members and no proof. A marketplace with two sides has to fill one market before it advertises anywhere. Start with Central Arkansas.

What it is: a link-in-bio page each creator can customize, plus two things others don't have: a section for open briefs and a wall of logos from brands they've worked with. Creators pay $29.99/month, businesses $199.99/month, with annual plans for both. Matching is swipe-style.

  • Build the smallest version that's still worth paying for: the creator page, the logo wall and open briefs. Swipe matching can come right after. The roadmap's rule: do fewer things well.
  • Seed briefs before you recruit creators. Creators pay because briefs pay out. Every consultative and tier-two client gets a business seat and posts briefs into the network, so creators have paid work waiting on day one.
  • Recruit a founding group of 50 creators in Central Arkansas by direct outreach (Instagram DMs, Creator Day alumni, @atlocalcentralar creators). Run the Rule of 100: 100 creator touches a day until the group is full.
  • Trade margin for proof on purpose: a founding-member rate for early creators, with written conditions: a testimonial video, permission to show their page in ads, and their first brief result.
  • Let creators build the page before they pay. It's a trial that shows the value. They pay to go live and unlock open briefs.
  • Start a waitlist and email list now, one for creators and one for businesses, and email each one weekly.
  • Film the swipe. A creator swiping through real local briefs is the clearest demo and the hook for ads.

The cancellation rates and the 85% margin are guesses. Replace them with real numbers once members are paying.

  • Keep paid ads for creators under about $105 per paying member. If they cost more, recruit creators through outreach and referrals.
  • Sell business seats through people first: consultative deals, tier-two clients and outreach. At around $1,400 per business, sales calls pay for themselves.
  • Push annual plans to bring cash in early. Annual prices are still to be set.
  • Check the growth ceiling each month: new members per month × lifetime revenue. For example, 40 creators a month = $15K/mo, and 10 businesses a month = $50K/mo.
  • Cut cancellations by keeping it simple: a quick win in the first 48 hours (page live, first brief seen) and a personal welcome.

3Consultative sales: the engine that funds everything

Jamie and Heath sell big consulting engagements. These deals pay for the network. The channel is outreach, worked every day.

  • Split the Rule of 100 between Jamie and Heath: 100 combined touches a day to the best-fit list. Log each one, every day.
  • Aim at the best 20% of clients: healthcare, foundations, energy and minerals, and businesses with several locations. Put about 5x the effort there.
  • Build every deal into the network: each engagement includes business seats and a set number of briefs. The client gets creators, and creators get paid work.
  • Keep taking payment at signing. Cash up front is what pays for the network.
  • Check pricing against close rate: closing more than 60% of proposals means we're underpriced by 2 to 3x.
  • Pitch the ongoing monthly tier halfway through delivery, not at the end.
  • Write the proposal-deck process down (clone the engine, rebrand, demo, publish) so a custom deck takes hours.
  • Book one speaking slot a month. The existing talk and sign-up page are reusable.

4Tier-two products: Discovery LaunchPad, Creator Day, The Handoff

These are the offers in the middle: lower-priced ways in for businesses that aren't ready for a big engagement, and the step up partway through one.

  • Discovery LaunchPad = the paid version of what the ads teach: claiming the business in chat and showing up in answer engines. It's the natural next step after the AI Answer Check.
  • Creator Day = proof and a month of content. Every Creator Day puts creators into the network and brings in a business seat.
  • The Handoff = the playbook for teams that want to do it themselves. Offer a network membership after it so they keep getting creators.
  • Put all three in one ladder on the site and in every proposal: AI Answer Check, then LaunchPad, then Creator Day, then a consulting engagement, then an ongoing membership.

5Local creative (@atlocalcentralar): the proof factory

Local client work is where proof gets made. It's also the template for the regional studios to copy. Capture what's already happening instead of making extra content.

  • Film every shoot twice: the client deliverable and the behind-the-scenes. The behind-the-scenes footage feeds @atlocal.media ads and case studies.
  • Post daily from captured footage only, with no extra shoots. Use the same few creator questions at every shoot so each answer works as a clip on its own.
  • Turn wins into case studies with business numbers (calls, bookings, foot traffic, leads), not views. Get the testimonial at the moment of the win: "Where were you before, and where are you now?"
  • Show the network in action: tag the creators, show their atLocal pages, and show the brief they were booked from.
  • Ask every local client for a Google review of atLocal, and keep one wins folder for all of it.

6@atlocal.media ads: make businesses want to work with us

The ads should leave a business owner thinking, "I need that, and atLocal knows how." They're built on three themes, with every ad pointing to the AI Answer Check or a booked call.

ThemeWhat the viewer learnsAd ideas
High trust videosWhy an unscripted video of real people beats a polished adBefore-and-after of a client; scripted vs. real creator, side by side

  • Test for free first: post 5 a week across the three themes, see which angles land, then put money only behind the winners.
  • Make lots of versions of each ad: write 20+ opening hooks, 3 to 5 main sections and 3 calls to action, then mix them. When one wins, make variations (new opener, new presenter, new background) before looking for a new angle.
  • One angle and one format per ad set, 1 to 4 ads each. Keep test budgets separate from the budget for scaling winners.
  • Have Meta optimize for booked calls, not page visits or opt-ins.
  • Kill rule: if an ad's cost passes 3x the target cost per booked call, turn it off. Split the budget 70% on proven winners, 30% on new tests.
  • Every Monday, ask two questions: is the cost per booked call normal, and is the number of calls normal?
  • Never promise placement in AI answers. Say "how you're found and mentioned," not "we'll get you ranked."

7First 30 days

The first 30 days do four things: lock in funding, seed briefs, recruit founding creators and start testing ads.

  • Pull last year's numbers: deals, proposals sent, close rate, average deal size, where each client came from.
  • Block 100 minutes a day each for Jamie and Heath for consultative outreach.
  • Write the brief template and get current clients to post the first briefs.
  • Open the founding-creator waitlist and start 100 creator DMs a day in Central Arkansas.
  • Build the AI Answer Check as a one-page report.
  • Shoot the first 10 @atlocal.media videos across the three themes, then boost the top 2.
  • Start the Monday report: outreach touches, booked calls, cost per booked call, creators on the waitlist, briefs posted.

8Don't do yet

Each of these skips a stage, and the roadmap says skipping stages wastes money.

  • Launching the app outside Central Arkansas before the first market has enough creators and briefs.
  • Paid ads for creator sign-ups before the founding group has proof (testimonials, booked briefs).
  • Charging businesses $199.99 before there are enough creators for them to choose from.
  • Building every feature before briefs are flowing.
  • Adding new channels before outreach and @atlocal.media bring in leads every week for 8+ weeks.
  • Judging ads by views instead of booked calls.

The one move

Seed briefs from current clients, then recruit 50 founding creators in Central Arkansas, while Jamie and Heath run 100 outreach touches a day to fund it.
